# Thinking Lenses

Purpose: apply six programming-derived thinking lenses as a silent self-check on the thinking process itself — reduce ambiguity and interpretation gaps in both reasoning and the resulting deliverable. Applies to the thinking process on every task. Framework-independent: a general-purpose thinking pattern, not tied to any one skill.

## The six lenses

1. **Sequence（順序）** — Is the order of steps/events unambiguous and complete? Is that order actually required, or could steps be reordered or run independently?
2. **If-Then（条件分岐）** — Are all decision branches and their conditions exhaustive (MECE)? Has a conclusion been reached without stating the condition it depends on?
3. **Loop（反復）** — Does any repeated step have a stated start and exit condition? Is refinement open-ended when it shouldn't be?
4. **Logic tree（演繹/帰納）** — Is each conclusion tagged, even mentally, as deductive (necessarily follows from stated premises) or inductive (inferred from grouped evidence, falsifiable)? Are sibling branches MECE?
5. **Parallel（並列）** — Are independent items forced into an artificial single-file order? Are sibling items at the same level actually the same kind of thing (logical/lingual parallelism)?
6. **Sync/Async（同期/非同期）** — Does the next step genuinely need to wait for this one's result, or can it proceed while this one completes in the background?

## Scope

- Mental check only. Never write these six labels into a deliverable unless the deliverable already has a natural place for them (an argument document via `minto-*`, a dependency-ordered task list via Spec Kit, Given/When/Then acceptance criteria via `clarifier`/`coder`).
- Framework-independent: usable on any complex reasoning, not only when a specific skill is active. Composes with `rules/clarifier.md` (apply after ambiguity is resolved, as a reasoning-quality check).
