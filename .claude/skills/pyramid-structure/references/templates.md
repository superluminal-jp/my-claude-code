# Shapes

Use a shape only when it helps the reader; never force headings onto a short answer.

## Answer
1. Answer the controlling question directly.
2. First-level claims that justify it, in one order.
3. Evidence, examples, calculations or caveats nested under the claim each supports.
4. A next step only when the user needs one. No closing summary that repeats the opening.

## Decision
1. Recommendation: one option and why it wins on the decisive criteria.
2. Decisive criteria only.
3. 2 to 4 realistic options at the same level.
4. Trade-offs: evidence-backed pros, cons, uncertainty, reversibility.
5. Material risks and mitigations.
6. Next action (owner, action, timeframe) when requested or operationally needed.

The recommendation synthesizes the comparison; do not leave the reader to infer it from a feature list.

## Long document
- Open with the governing answer or executive conclusion when the genre allows.
- Each section lead states the section's conclusion, not its topic; each subsection supports its parent.
- Peer sections share abstraction level and one order.
- Evidence, methods, appendices and implementation detail go to the lowest useful level.
- Use situation, complication, question only when needed to establish the question.
- Where a genre fixes another order, follow it and keep the dependency structure (see genres.md).
