# Core rules

## Invariant

Structure substantive communication as:

> controlling question → governing answer → supporting claims → evidence/detail

Every child must answer the question raised by its parent. Every parent must accurately synthesize its children.

## Vertical logic

For each parent-child relation:

- determine the question the parent creates;
- require the children to answer that question directly;
- remove or relocate children that do not materially support the parent.

## Horizontal logic

Sibling points must:

- be logically comparable;
- belong to one defensible grouping;
- use one ordering principle;
- collectively support the parent.

Use one of these ordering logics when applicable:

- deductive;
- inductive;
- chronological;
- structural;
- ranked importance.

Do not mix ordering logics within one sibling group without an explicit reason.

## Synthesis

A parent heading or lead sentence must state the implication of its children, not merely name their topic.

Weak:

- Reasons
- Findings
- Risks
- Considerations

Strong:

- Retention, not acquisition, is the primary source of the revenue decline.
- Option B meets the deadline while minimizing irreversible risk.
- Three independent constraints make the current design infeasible.

## Completeness and overlap

Prefer collectively sufficient and non-overlapping sibling groups when the subject admits such a partition. Do not force artificial MECE structure when reality is overlapping or incomplete; state the limitation instead.

## SCQ

Use Situation–Complication–Question only when the reader needs context to understand why the controlling question matters.

- Situation: accepted context.
- Complication: change, tension, or problem.
- Question: the decision or issue to resolve.
- Answer: the governing answer.

Do not surface SCQ mechanically as headings unless that form helps the user.

## Reasoning privacy

The pyramid constrains the structure of reasoning; it does not require disclosure of hidden chain-of-thought.

Externalize only what is useful and appropriate:

- answer;
- decisive reasons;
- evidence;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- alternatives/trade-offs when material;
- verification or test results.
