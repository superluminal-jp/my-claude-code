# Frameworks for the six sections

Read when eliciting a section. Apply only a framework listed here, and name its source when it is used.

| Section | Framework and source | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Vision and problem | Business Model Canvas: Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation*, Wiley, 2010 | The overall shape of the business |
| Vision and problem | Lean Canvas: Ash Maurya, *Running Lean*, 2nd ed., O'Reilly, 2012 | A leaner treatment for an early product |
| Vision and problem | Marty Cagan, *INSPIRED*, 2nd ed., Wiley, 2017/2018 | Keeping strategy apart from a roadmap or backlog in a software product |
| Vision and problem | Michael E. Porter, "What Is Strategy?," *Harvard Business Review* 74(6), 1996 | The definition of strategy itself: a distinct set of activities |
| Target users | Jobs to Be Done: Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon & David S. Duncan, "Know Your Customers' 'Jobs to Be Done'," *Harvard Business Review* 94(9), 2016 | Why a user would adopt the product |
| Target users | ISO 9241-210:2019, *Ergonomics of human-system interaction - Human-centred design for interactive systems* | How user context is gathered and validated |
| Target users | Opportunity Solution Tree: Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*, Product Talk LLC, 2021 | Connecting the user's reason to what is elicited |
| Value proposition | Value Proposition Canvas: Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Gregory Bernarda & Alan Smith, *Value Proposition Design*, Wiley, 2014 | Matching the product to the user's gains and pains |
| Success metrics | OKRs: John Doerr, *Measure What Matters*, Portfolio/Penguin, 2018; tracing to Andrew S. Grove, *High Output Management*, Random House, 1983 | Outcome-oriented objectives |
| Success metrics | Balanced Scorecard: Robert S. Kaplan & David P. Norton, "The Balanced Scorecard," *Harvard Business Review* 70(1), 1992 | A product with several stakeholders, where one guiding metric would misrepresent it |
| Success metrics | North Star metric: named by Sean Ellis in the early 2010s; systematized by Amplitude and John Cutler, "The North Star Playbook" | One guiding metric |
| Scope and priority | MoSCoW: Dai Clegg & Richard Barker, *Case Method Fast-Track: A RAD Approach*, Addison-Wesley, 1994 | The in-or-out call |
| Scope and priority | Kano model: Noriaki Kano, Nobuhiko Seraku, Fumio Takahashi & Shin-ichi Tsuji, "Attractive Quality and Must-Be Quality," *Quality* 14(2), 1984, pp. 39-48 | Optionally, whether an in-scope item is a baseline expectation or a differentiator |

## Considered and left out of the fixed set

Available to cite as an extension, not required in every brief:

- Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*, Cafepress, 2005, and Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup*, Crown Business, 2011: customer development and build-measure-learn. They concern validating a brief's hypotheses, a later activity this skill does not perform.
- Marc Andreessen, "The Only Thing That Matters," *pmarchive*, 2007: product-market-fit vocabulary, not a structured elicitation method.
